In the age where ai tools can build just about anything you can imagine, the last bottleneck for businesses is ironically the first one: *creativity. *****Humans have always been able to achieve any goal they set their mind to. We harnessed fire, water, and the wind. And somehow we got to using tiny things we cant see that we break apart to release an insane amount of energy which (depending on your local grid) powers the device that you are reading or listening on right now. Tell a person 100 years ago that everyone would have all the knowledge in the world in the palm of their hands they would call you a crazy person. 400 years ago they would burn you at the stake. And 3000 years ago they'd say: "𓅓𓂋𓏏𓏭𓏏𓏤𓏥". What these examples all have in common is that they thought it impossible, but here we are. 



### The Bannister effect

![A long line of hikers waiting for their turn to get to the summit of everest](/images/image.png)

There is a phenomenon known as the "Bannister effect" in which the amount of people who achieve a goal rapidly increases once a pioneer breaks a barrier. Everest used to be unclimbable, now there are lines of people waiting for their turn to take pictures at the top and get that satisfying Strava screen for their instagram stories for the rest of the month. Or doing an Ironman used to be the pinnacle achievement for a multi sport athlete but now it seems like everyones boss, co-worker, or sister that has no business being that cracked has done one or more. The Bannister effect is active proof that us humans will fill the mold that we set ourselves. Now there are two types of people which respond very differently when they see someone do something they previously thought impossible. One thinks "I could never do that." And the other thinks "Why not me?"



### "Why not me?"

Why not? Really? Have you asked yourself that? If your reasoning boils down to "I'm just not someone who says 'why not.'" Then allow me to get real meta here: why not try saying "why not?" Our minds are like water, we fill the mold we give ourselves so why not just give yourself the biggest, dopest, most amazing mold possible? You should take this same principle to how you build software. AI tools allow us to write any software in any stack and in a fraction of the time. It is like if Einstein spoke 100 languages, had 6mg zyns, and access to Huberman's nootropic stack. You can make a personal tool that no one in the world will use but it replaces a 5 dollar monthly budgeting tool; or you can build an enterprise grade B2B software that lands you $40k/month contracts for a couple months until one of your clients builds the same thing 1% better and puts you out of business. You can quite literally build anything you can conjure up.



### Taste, creativity, and AI-driven development.

I know it is tempting to ask your favorite LLM "Hey what can I make look better about this site" or "how can i make this blog post more engaging" but please I beg you for the sake of yourself and your audience: DO NOT OUTSOURCE YOUR CREATIVITY AND TASTE. I repeat: I know it is **tempting** but your creativity and taste is one of the things that makes you you . I am guilty of it in the past and I always end up getting frustrated or just being unhappy with the results. It might not happen in the moment but I will come back to a project a month later and say "Wow this looks like shit. Who the hell made this!"
